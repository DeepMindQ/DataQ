Network Operators (CSPs)

A communications operator sells connectivity, and connectivity is the only product where quality is measured in milliseconds by machines the customer never sees. The estate that delivers it — RAN, transport, core, IT — generates petabytes of telemetry a day; the business systems that monetize it — CRM, charging, billing, wholesale settlement — generate their own. The margin problem is that the two never meet at decision time: the NOC reacts to alarms instead of predicting failures, retention offers fire on calendars instead of on intent, wholesale revenue leaks through settlement gaps, and energy, the second cost line, runs uninstrumented per site. This page is the engineering picture of that gap — the systems an operator actually runs, the eight places where money leaks, and the six deep builds Dataq Software ships to close them.

How a network operator actually runs day to day

In carrier operations, the network already knows more than the business acts on. Every morning the NOC inherits the night's alarm backlog: element managers and network probes have streamed thousands of events into the fault-management system, correlation has done what its rules allow, and the duty engineers triage the rest by age and severity. Tickets route to the field-force system, crews work by dispatch windows, and customer impact is usually discovered when care volume spikes — the IVR loading a known-outage message after the third thousandth call, not before. Performance assurance runs in parallel on its own dashboards, tuned for capacity reviews rather than for the decisions happening at 3 a.m.

The commercial side runs on BSS plumbing that predates most of the products it sells. Orders flow from the storefront through product catalog, CRM and orchestration to activation, ideally through TM Forum Open APIs — TMF620 for product catalog, TMF640 for service activation, TMF641 for service ordering — though in practice every operator owns a few hand-rolled bridges the vendors never quite replaced. Mediation collects network events and turns them into chargeable records; rating and charging price them; billing assembles the invoice. Care agents swivel across the CRM, the billing GUI and the network status page to answer one question. Meanwhile wholesale teams exchange interconnect and roaming files with counterparties, dispute the differences, and book the leakage as margin variance.

The quieter costs accumulate outside both views. Energy meters at tower and exchange sites feed utility invoices but not decision systems, so per-site waste is invisible until someone walks the site. Fraud — IRSF through premium-rate destinations, Wangiri callbacks, PBX hacking, SIM-box bypass of international transit — consumes capacity and settlement goodwill while the fraud team fights with static rules. Asset registers drift from what is actually installed. None of this appears on a single dashboard, which is precisely why it survives year after year: the operator's problems are not a lack of data, they are decisions that never reach the data.

Where the margin leaks

Ask operator operations and finance leaders where the money leaks and eight answers recur, described the same way whether the estate is mobile, fixed or cable. Dataq Software's diagnostic scores each leak in your own telemetry before recommending a build, because the leak that dominates in one operator is often minor in the next.

  • The NOC reacts instead of predicting

    Alarm correlation tuned years ago suppresses noise unevenly, so engineers triage by age rather than by business impact. Customer-impacting events are found by ticket surges, degradation runs to failure because performance baselines never reach the fault wall, and every preventable outage bills itself twice — once in repair, once in the churn it seeds.

  • Churn saves run on campaign calendars

    Retention offers fire in monthly batches while intent signals — care contacts, usage drops, billing disputes, top-up failures, competitive SIM swaps — sit unwatched in systems the calendar never reads. By the time the campaign fires, the customer has decided, so the offer subsidizes departures that were preventable and misses the ones that were not.

  • Wholesale settlement leaks by design

    Interconnect and roaming reconcile through bilateral file exchange with evidence each side assembles independently. Mis-rated routes, unbillable destinations, short-payments and test-call gaps surface as margin variance, and the dispute cycle takes quarters because neither side can query the other's evidence.

  • Fraud evolves faster than the rules

    IRSF through premium-rate destinations, Wangiri callback schemes, PBX hacking and SIM-box bypass adapt to static thresholds within weeks. The fraud team's rules over-block legitimate traffic and still miss the novel scheme, and every case file — call detail records, route evidence, settlement impact — is assembled by hand under time pressure.

  • Energy is a second P&L nobody owns

    RAN and datacenter energy is among the largest opex lines, yet per-site telemetry cannot separate load-driven consumption from waste: rectifier faults, idle legacy equipment, overcooled shelters, generators running against grid schedules. ESG reporting reconstructs the picture quarterly from invoices instead of running on telemetry.

  • Care deflects without resolving

    The IVR contains, the chatbot deflects, and the same customer calls back twice. Containment metrics improve while cost per contact rises and CSAT falls, because self-service answers questions the account data could have prevented and escalations arrive at the agent stripped of context.

  • Activation integrity erodes silently

    Inventory drifts from as-built reality one provisioning exception at a time, until activation failures, wrongful disconnections and FTTH rollout slippage all trace to records nobody trusts. Every reconciliation is manual, and the delta between ordered, provisioned and billed services becomes the quiet tax on every new launch.

  • Asset and site economics are folklore

    Tower leases, spare parts, legacy nodes and truck rolls live in separate registers that never reconcile to finance. Decommissioning decisions wait on data that exists in fragments, so legacy estates keep burning energy and lease payments years after the traffic moved.

Across all eight leaks the pattern holds: the estate already produces the evidence, but the decision never reaches it — correlation lacks impact context, saves lack intent signals, settlement lacks queryable records, energy lacks baselines. That is why the first build is the telemetry backbone, not another rules engine.

How Dataq Software works with network operators

Dataq Software builds inside the operator estate you already run. We do not arrive with a replacement OSS, a rip-and-replace BSS migration, or a strategy deck that ends where the NOC floor begins. The engagement model is engineering-first: a two-week diagnostic that baselines one workflow end to end — assurance, retention, wholesale, fraud, energy or care — then a scoped build that ships behind your change process, your TM Forum Open API surface and the third-party registers your resilience regime requires.

Three engineering commitments shape everything we deliver in this sub-industry. First, every automated decision carries replay: inputs, feature values, model versions, policy rules and outcomes are stored so that a disputed dispatch, a contested save offer or a flagged fraud case is a query, not an investigation. Second, every decision respects the operational boundary — the build advises and executes through your OSS and BSS interfaces, with human checkpoints where the network, the customer contract or the regulator demands one. Third, every build lands with its evidence layer: the artifacts an auditor, a counterparty or a regulator will ask for are generated continuously by the system, not assembled by a team the week before the review.

The commercial model follows the same discipline. Where the metric is clean — churn in a target segment, energy per site, care cost per contact — we contract against the delta with the baseline locked during the diagnostic; where the metric is confounded, the feasibility read says so and the scope is fixed-fee instead. The published record in this sub-industry is the deepest in the sector: churn down 19% in target segments at a tier-1 European operator through an event-triggered save hub, energy per site down 18% across 12,000 sites at a Middle East operator group through telemetry-driven optimization, and care cost per contact down 36% at a North American cable operator through a resolution-first conversational platform.

The builds: six deep engineering services for network operators

The catalog below is the whole of our operator practice. Each build is named for the workflow it changes, sized in the diagnostic, and shipped with the controls the operator's regulator and counterparties expect. None of them requires replacing an OSS or BSS; all of them require taking the data contracts seriously.

1. NOC assurance and AIOps — from alarm flood to decision queue

The build turns the fault wall from a list into a queue of decisions. Change-data-capture and stream ingestion land element, probe and ticket events into one estate; event correlation clusters them into incidents with probable causes learned from resolution history; noise models suppress the flapping majority; and performance baselines feed predictive maintenance so degradation is worked before it becomes an outage. Every incident carries customer-impact mapping — which services, which segments, which enterprise contracts — so the NOC prioritizes by business consequence instead of by alarm age.

The action wiring is where assurance programs usually fail, so the build treats it as first-class: incidents dispatch to field force with evidence attached, trigger proactive care messages and known-outage content before call volume spikes, and feed post-incident reviews with a timeline reconstructed from the same telemetry. Ticket triage runs through the agentic layer — classification, enrichment, deduplication and evidence assembly — with engineers deciding, not collecting. The measurable deltas are alarm-to-incident precision, mean time to repair and the ticket backlog the NOC stops generating.

2. Event-triggered churn and ARPU hub

The build moves saves from the campaign calendar to the event stream. Intent signals — care contacts and their resolutions, usage drops, billing disputes, top-up failures, SIM-change and port-out indicators — fire the decision service in the moment. Eligibility, propensity, vulnerability flags and consumer-protection constraints are evaluated per event, and the offer engine returns a save action inside the policy envelope your commercial team owns: channel, discount depth, contract terms and disclosure requirements all encoded as rules the optimizer cannot violate.

ARPU management runs on the same hub: plan-fit recommendations, upsell eligibility and price-change exposure computed from the same features, so retention and growth decisions never contradict each other. Champion and challenger policies run on shadow traffic through the gates your governance defines, saves are measured against held-out controls rather than campaign decks, and the published record shows the ceiling: churn down 19% in target segments at a tier-1 European operator, with the save-rate lift attributed to the hub by design rather than by argument.

3. Revenue assurance and fraud defense on wholesale flows

The build attacks leakage where it lives: between the network events that happened and the settlement records that claim they did. Call detail streams land alongside interconnect and roaming settlement files, and reconciliation runs continuously rather than at invoicing cutoff — rated-against-counted per route, per destination, per counterparty, with TAP file matching and IOT application checked record by record. Every discrepancy becomes a case with its evidence pre-assembled: CDRs, route proofs, rate cards and prior settlements.

Fraud defense layers on the same stream: IRSF and Wangiri detection scored on destination-risk graphs and call-pattern anomalies, PBX-hacking signatures watched on enterprise trunks, SIM-box bypass detected through route-quality and device-correlation signals. The fraud workbench gives investigators linked evidence instead of raw alerts, and every automated block carries replay — a contested block against an enterprise customer is answered with its inputs, not with an apology. The measurable deltas are leakage identified per wholesale billing cycle, false-positive rate on legitimate traffic and case cycle time.

4. Site-level energy intelligence

The build makes energy a managed asset instead of an invoice line. Meter, rectifier, generator, temperature and grid-schedule telemetry lands per site into the lakehouse; baselines are computed per site class, load band and climate profile; and anomalies are classified by cause — rectifier faults, idle legacy equipment, overcooling, generator mis-scheduling, theft. The optimization loop then acts: equipment work orders generated with evidence, cooling and generator policies adjusted within network-availability constraints, and legacy nodes queued for decommissioning when traffic and energy agree they are finished.

Because energy decisions compete with availability, the build encodes the constraints explicitly: no optimization may violate the resilience envelope, and every action carries its measured result — kWh before and after, normalized per bit delivered. ESG reporting runs from the same telemetry rather than from quarterly invoice reconstruction. The published record shows the ceiling: energy per site down 18% across 12,000 sites at a Middle East operator group, with the reduction measured against locked per-site baselines.

5. Resolution-first conversational care

The care build is retrieval-grounded and deliberately not a deflection machine. Voice and chat agents answer from a permission-aware index of your knowledge base, product terms and the customer's own records — balance, contract, open tickets, local network status — and transactional intents hand off to governed APIs by policy. Known-outage detection feeds the assistant before the queue forms: the platform reads incident telemetry, so the customer who calls about the fault gets the fault's status, not a scripted menu.

The evaluation harness is part of the deliverable: golden question sets per intent, containment scored against post-contact outcomes rather than deflection alone, escalation-context quality measured on what the agent receives when the bot hands over. Care cost falls because first contact resolves, not because second contact is hidden. The published record shows the ceiling: care cost per contact down 36% at a North American cable operator, with CSAT tracked alongside cost in the same telemetry.

6. Activation, inventory and rollout integrity

The build reconciles the three ledgers that must agree — ordered, provisioned and billed — and keeps them agreeing. Orders, orchestration events and activation results land through TMF-class Open APIs and their legacy bridges; the inventory spine is conformed to the SID model with as-built corrections captured from the field; and drift detection flags every resource whose records disagree before it becomes a wrongful disconnection or a stuck FTTH order. Provisioning failures route to a triage queue with the evidence attached rather than to care.

Rollout programs get the same discipline: FTTH and 5G deployment tracking joins site surveys, civil-works milestones, equipment installs and activation results into one measurable pipeline, so the weekly rollout review reads system truth instead of spreadsheet optimism. The measurable deltas are provisioning failure rate, order-to-activate cycle time, inventory record accuracy and the rollout slippage the program office stops discovering late.

The data plane: from OSS silos to a governed telemetry estate

Every decision gap in an operator traces back to a data gap, and most data gaps trace back to the same architecture habit: element managers write vendor files, BSS batches overnight, settlement exchanges flat files, and decisions run on whatever the last load knew. The data plane build inverts the pattern where it matters. Telemetry, CDRs, orders, settlement records and site power data land through change-data-capture and governed interfaces into one lakehouse in near real time; the overnight batch keeps running for finance and regulatory reporting, but decision surfaces stop depending on it.

Governance is engineered, not asserted. Every feed carries a schema contract with a named owner, a freshness SLA and a reconciliation check against the source system's own controls — mediation totals against network counts, settlement lines against rated CDRs, inventory against as-built surveys. The subscriber and resource masters apply survivorship rules your data owners sign, and every downstream consumer — assurance features, save-hub eligibility, fraud scoring, energy baselines — reads through the contract layer so a schema change fails loudly at the contract instead of silently inside a model.

  • Network and BSS stream ingestion

    change-data-capture from element managers, probes, mediation, CRM and care systems with exactly-once semantics, keeping decision surfaces current without touching batch settlement or finance

  • SID-conformed inventory spine

    network inventory conformed to the SID information model, with as-built corrections from the field captured as first-class events rather than spreadsheet patches

  • Settlement as queryable records

    interconnect and roaming files parsed into structured, counterparty-keyed records so reconciliation and disputes become queries instead of evidence-hunting exercises

  • Contract-enforced freshness

    per-feed SLAs with alerting and automated breach reports, so a late mediation load pages an owner instead of silently skewing fraud features and revenue reports

The decision layer: correlation, saves and settlement with replay

Operator decisions carry operational and regulatory tails: a fraud block interrupts paying customers, a save offer carries consumer-protection obligations, an energy work order competes with network availability, a settlement claim commits the operator to a counterparty dispute. The decision layer treats those tails as first-class requirements. Every decision records its inputs, feature values, model versions, policy rules and reason codes in a replay store that answers audit questions as queries — when an enterprise customer, a counterparty or a regulator asks why, the answer is retrieved, not reconstructed.

Champion and challenger governance is built in rather than bolted on: correlation policies, save strategies and fraud thresholds run as challengers on shadow traffic with performance tracked in the registry your governance function owns, promotions pass through the gates your change process already defines, and rollbacks are rehearsed, not improvised. The same service layer hosts assurance, saves, fraud and energy decisions so the governance surface is one registry, one replay store, one set of gates — instead of four platforms with four audit stories.

The agentic layer: digital workers for the queues nobody clears

The backlog work in an operator — ticket triage, fraud case assembly, settlement dispute preparation, provisioning-failure investigation, energy anomaly verification — follows patterns an agentic workforce can absorb without touching judgment authority. Dataq Software's agent platform runs digital workers with explicit role scopes: the tools they may call, the systems they may read, the actions that require a human checkpoint and the budget of work per run. Every run logs its steps, tool calls and outputs to the same evidence store the human workflow uses, so an agent's case file is auditable exactly like an engineer's.

Where generative agents add leverage — summarizing incident timelines, drafting settlement dispute files, compiling fraud evidence packs, drafting known-outage communications — the pattern is grounded generation with citation obligations and human authorship of record. Agents never activate a service, never block a route, never commit a settlement position; they move the work to the decision point and stop. The capacity story is measured in cleared cases per engineer per day, which is how NOC and operations leaders justify these programs upward.

Integration engineering: the estate stays, the contracts change

Operator estates are layered over decades of mergers, vendor cycles and interim solutions, and the fastest way to fail a program is to promise greenfield. Our integration posture is contractual: we define schema contracts with each system owner, land data through CDC, mediation taps and governed APIs, write back through the TMF-class interfaces, workflow hooks and dispatch paths your change process already controls, and keep every new component independently deployable behind feature flags. OSS suites are not replaced; they are supplied with better context and drained of the decision work they were never good at.

The vendor chain gets the same treatment. Element managers, mediation vendors, fraud screening services and field-force platforms sit behind abstraction layers with contract tests, so a vendor swap is a configuration change with a migration runbook rather than a re-platform. Where a legacy component must stay, the offload pattern applies: the batch window keeps posting, while decision surfaces read from the stream the offload produces. This is unglamorous work and it is why the builds hold up in production instead of in a pilot lab.

  • TMF-class write-back paths

    activation updates, trouble tickets and dispatch actions flow through the Open API interfaces and legacy bridges your change process already governs, with contract tests on every dependency

  • Mediation and probe taps

    telemetry landed from mediation taps and probe feeds rather than element-manager forklifts, so the assurance build never negotiates with an OSS release calendar

  • Abstraction over vendor APIs

    element, fraud-screening and field-force vendors behind one interface each, so a renewal or a swap is configuration with a runbook, not surgery

  • Feature-flagged, independently deployable

    every build ships behind flags with rehearsed rollback, because the change advisory board and the resilience regulator will both ask

How an engagement runs

The engagement shape is deliberately short on ceremony and long on measurement. It starts with the two-week diagnostic: one workflow baselined end to end, the leak quantified in your own telemetry, the data contracts inventoried, and a written feasibility read you can circulate — including the uncomfortable findings. If the delta is real, the build is scoped against the gates your change and governance processes define; if it is not, you keep the measurement design and the baseline either way.

  • Weeks 1-2: diagnostic and baseline

    workflow instrumentation, leak quantification, contract inventory and a feasibility read with the delta estimate and the confounders named

  • Weeks 3-8: first vertical slice

    one lane live behind flags with replay and evidence running from day one — for assurance that is one incident family; for retention it is one trigger class; for energy it is one site class

  • Weeks 9-16: gates, controls, expansion

    governance documentation landed, QC sampling live, remaining lanes added, capacity and unit-cost telemetry reporting to the operations review

  • Quarterly: challenger reviews

    correlation, save and fraud challengers evaluated in the registry, treatment strategies re-optimized, the baseline re-verified against controls

Delivery runs in your delivery rhythm, not a parallel one: our engineers work in your repositories or a shared workspace with the same CI, the same security review and the same access model your teams use. Knowledge transfer is a gate condition, not a closing slide — by the expansion phase your engineers are shipping changes to the builds, because the runbooks, contracts and evaluation suites are theirs from week one.

The team and the operating rhythm

An operator build team is small and senior: a lead engineer who has run decision systems at carrier scale, data engineers who own the CDC and contract layer, ML engineers who own scoring and evaluation, a controls engineer who owns the evidence layer, and domain leads who have worked NOC floors, wholesale desks and rollout programs in production estates. Agentic-workflow specialists join for the triage and evidence-assembly builds. The team works to your release calendar and your gate vocabulary from the first week.

The rhythm is the same at every client: weekly operations review on the workflow's own telemetry, fortnightly gate submissions to change and governance, quarterly challenger reviews, and an incident practice that treats the decision store and the evidence layer as production-critical systems — because by the second quarter, they are. Escalations go to named engineers, not to a queue.

Failure modes we design against

Operator programs fail in known ways, and most of the failure modes are organizational rather than mathematical. We name them in the feasibility read and design against them explicitly, because a program that cannot survive its second quarter is not worth starting.

  • Correlation without an action path

    incidents cluster beautifully and change nothing because dispatch, care and comms were never wired — the build ships its action integrations with its models, or it does not ship

  • Pilot purgatory

    a demo built on extracted data that never integrates — prevented by shipping the first vertical slice through your change process, flags and gates included, in the first two months

  • Save offers that break the rules

    retention discounts issued outside policy or to customers who must not receive them — consumer-protection constraints are hard rules in the decision service, not review checklists

  • Energy dashboards without loops

    per-site telemetry that reports and does not act — optimization is wired to work orders and policies within availability constraints, and measured in kWh, not in slide charts

  • Inventory drift re-accumulating

    records reconciled once and left to rot — as-built capture, drift detection and correction loops run continuously, or the activation build decays within a year

  • Evidence debt

    audit preparation consuming a team every quarter because artifacts were manual — the evidence layer generates them continuously or the build is not done

Questions operator leaders ask us

  • Do we need to replace our OSS first?

    No. Every build consumes streams from the OSS and writes back through TMF-class interfaces and your dispatch paths. The assurance suite stays; it stops being the bottleneck for decisions.

  • Will this fight our existing AIOps vendor?

    We build the decision layer, not a duplicate event pipe. Correlation and triage read from your existing feeds, and the abstraction layer means the vendor underneath can change without the decisions changing.

  • How do saves avoid regulator trouble?

    Consumer-protection constraints — disclosure, eligibility, vulnerability flags, offer depth limits — are hard rules inside the decision service, and every offer decision is logged with its basis for review.

  • Can you work with our settlement formats?

    Yes. TAP and bilateral interconnect formats are parsed into counterparty-keyed records, and reconciliation runs continuously against CDR streams. Disputes start from evidence both sides can query.

  • What about energy data quality?

    Site telemetry arrives messy; the build profiles it first, sets achievable metering baselines, and treats data-quality work as part of the optimization, not as a precondition for it.

  • How is success measured without gaming?

    Baselines are locked during the diagnostic, holdouts are used where experiments are cheap, and every metric reports from system telemetry rather than a status deck. The feasibility read names the confounders first.

  • What is the smallest program worth running?

    One workflow, one baseline, one vertical slice — retention and assurance are the two most common starters because their signals are already instrumented. If the diagnostic cannot find the delta, we say so and stop.

  • Who owns the build after you leave?

    Your teams, with the runbooks, contracts, evaluation suites and registry entries transferred as gate conditions during expansion — not as a closing deliverable. Several programs run entirely in-house after the second quarter.
