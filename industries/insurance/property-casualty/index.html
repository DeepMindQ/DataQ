Property & Casualty

Property and casualty carriers live in two moments: pricing the risk before the event and handling the claim after it. Both moments are decision factories — one sets the rate against exposure, catastrophe models and filing-approved plans; the other settles against policy language, estimates and fraud signals under state unfair-claims clocks. Both reward carriers whose document and data machinery is industrialised, and both punish carriers who discover their pricing drift, estimate leakage or cat exposure blind spots a quarter after the money has gone. This page is the engineering picture of the P&C machine as Dataq Software sees it: what runs from FNOL to recovery, from submission to filing, where each stage leaks or invites exam findings, and the six deep builds that move cycle time, leakage and pricing adequacy with the evidence a rate reviewer or market-conduct examiner will ask for.

How the machine runs from quote to recovery

Property and casualty carriers run two decision factories, and the first one starts before the customer exists. A submission arrives through an agent, a digital channel or an MGA portal; the rating engine prices it against filing-approved rate plans, relativities and model scores, with telematics and enrichment data sharpening personal lines and judgment plus survey data pricing commercial. Bound policies flow into the policy administration platform, which bills, endorsements and renews. Commercial books layer delegated authority on top: MGAs and MGUs write under binding authorities, and their business returns as bordereaux that must reconcile against the treaties and programs behind them.

The second factory opens with FNOL. A claim arrives from an app, a call centre, an agent or a telematics crash alert; intake verifies coverage, assembles the first packet — policy record, prior claims, weather and peril context — and assignment rules route it by severity, skill and workload. Adjusters, field or desk, manage the loss: estimates flow from repairers through estimatics platforms, photographs feed photo-based estimating, medical records drive bodily injury files. Payment integrity tests every payment; SIU screens the suspicious; subrogation pursues the responsible third party. Catastrophe events compress weeks of this into days, and the surge decides both the loss ratio and the retention story.

Underneath both factories, the actuarial and finance engine runs on the same data: loss triangles and IBNR reserving, pricing adequacy monitored by cohort, reinsurance recoverables tracked against treaties, and IFRS 17 or statutory reporting assembled from the marts. Rate filings move through state DOI calendars with actuarial justification attached, and every filing reads the same experience data the pricing models consumed. When the filing evidence is reconstructed instead of generated, exam season becomes an expedition — and the drift the filing should have caught was visible in the data a year earlier.

Where the margin leaks

Ask P&C operations, claims and actuarial leaders where the money leaks and the answers converge on the same eight places, described in their own words. Dataq Software scores each of them in your estate during the diagnostic, because the leak that dominates a personal motor book is often minor in a commercial property program — and the exam risk rarely sits where the org chart expects it.

  • FNOL packets triaged days late

    Intake captures the claim, but the packet — policy record, prior claims, peril context, photographs — is assembled by hand, so triage waits and severity mis-assignment compounds into the wrong adjuster, the wrong reserve and the wrong customer promise. Cycle time and leakage both start here.

  • Estimate leakage hiding in variance

    Repair estimates arrive through estimatics platforms, but nobody benchmarks variance by repairer, claim type and supplement pattern at the decision point. Leakage compounds quietly — in supplements, in betterment disputes, in total-loss valuations — until a quarterly review finds it a quarter late.

  • Pricing drift detected only at renewal

    GLM and telematics models price beautifully, but adequacy monitoring runs on monthly extracts that reconcile by spreadsheet. The portfolio drifts for quarters before the loss ratio says so, and the filing that should respond arrives after the drift has become a trend.

  • Filing evidence reconstructed under exam

    Rate filings demand actuarial justification, and the justification is rebuilt from fragments every cycle because the pricing registry never held it. Exam questions become expeditions, and challenger models live in notebooks the model-risk function cannot see.

  • Cat surge capacity decided in the event

    Event footprints arrive as vendor file drops, exposure joins are manual, and resource pre-positioning is decided by judgment under pressure. First-contact times blow out, adjuster allocation chases the event instead of leading it, and reinsurer notification obligations are checked late.

  • SIU referrals tuned to last year's fraud

    Referral thresholds fire on volume, not on network signal, so organised patterns pass through and honest claims get flagged. Investigators collect evidence by hand, referral precision decays, and the false-positive cost lands on customers and complaint volumes.

  • Subrogation left on the table

    Recovery opportunities — responsible third parties, product defects, deductible recovery — are detected inconsistently at intake and pursued unevenly after. The recovery ledger under-collects by an amount nobody can state, because demand assembly is manual and evidence lives in five systems.

  • Delegated authority flown blind

    MGA and MGU programs write business the carrier sees only through bordereaux that arrive in formats and qualities nobody standardized. Authority limits, program loss ratios and audit sampling run on trust and annual review, and the bordereaux reconciliation effort is an expense ratio line item.

The pattern across all eight leaks is the one every diagnostic in this sub-industry finds: the inputs that decide the money — estimates, footprints, telematics, bordereaux — arrive as vendor files nobody owns, so the decisions downstream run without their context. Context is a data engineering problem before it is a modeling problem.

The builds: six deep engineering services for property and casualty

The catalog below is the whole of our P&C practice. Each build is named for the workflow it changes, sized in the diagnostic, and shipped with the controls its regulator will expect. None of them require replacing the claims core; every one of them requires taking the vendor data contracts seriously — because in this sub-industry the money moves on vendor files.

1. FNOL intake and claims triage — the first decision, engineered

The build replaces the intake choke with a decision pipeline: FNOL arrives from app, call centre, agent or telematics crash detection, and the pipeline verifies coverage against the policy record, assembles the first packet automatically — prior claims, peril and weather context, photographs, telematics signals — and runs triage: severity scoring, assignment rules balanced for skill and workload, and straight-through lanes for clean, low-severity claims that flow to resolution with decision replay attached. Cat events flag themselves at intake, because the peril context is already in the packet.

The adjuster console receives a complete, cited file instead of a queue number, and mis-assignment is monitored as a first-class metric — severity errors tracked back to the triage features that caused them. Every decision writes to the replay store with reason codes, so a conduct or unfair-claims question months later is a retrieval. In the published record, this build moved a top-5 global P&C insurer's claims triage 55% faster; the measurable deltas are FNOL-to-first-contact time, straight-through share and the severity error rate that drives everything downstream.

2. Estimate intelligence and repair network management — leakage, engineered out

The build puts the benchmark inside the decision: every estimate that flows through the estimatics platform is scored against parts, labour and time benchmarks by vehicle, peril and region, with variance flags routed to the reviewing adjuster at the moment of review instead of in a quarterly report. Photo-based estimating runs through the same pipeline, so image quality, line-item confidence and supplement risk are visible before approval. Repairer scorecards build from the same telemetry — cycle time, supplement rate, variance trend — with a dispute process so the scorecard is evidence, not opinion.

Leakage analytics run beside the workflow: supplement frequency by pattern, betterment and depreciation consistency, total-loss valuation against live market data, salvage timing. The review effort concentrates where the risk is — high-variance, high-severity, repeat-pattern claims — instead of spreading evenly across the queue. The measurable deltas are leakage recovery rate, supplement rate and cycle time inside the repair network, each reported from the pipeline rather than estimated in a spreadsheet.

3. Pricing adequacy and the rate filing evidence factory

The pricing build closes the gap between model sophistication and governance capacity. Exposure, claims and telematics features flow through the contract layer into a governed actuarial environment; adequacy is monitored by cohort and segment continuously, so drift is detected within the quarter rather than at renewal; and indicated rate changes carry their actuarial documentation generated from the same registry that holds the models, the validation results and the challenger performance. Filing packets assemble with justification attached, ready for the state DOI calendar.

The governance surface is the deliverable as much as the pricing: a model-risk registry with inventory, evaluation results, fairness reviews for protected-class proxies and drift monitoring, all generated during the build rather than reconstructed before an exam. Under the EU AI Act and state DOI guidance on pricing models, that documentation layer is a condition of operating, not an aspiration — and it is exactly what the evidence factory produces as a by-product of running the pricing process.

4. Catastrophe response and exposure accumulation — the surge, engineered

The cat build makes the event legible before it makes landfall: vendor cat model feeds and weather signals land through the contract layer, join to the exposure in force continuously, and produce event footprints with predicted claim volumes that drive resource pre-positioning — adjuster allocation, mobile FNOL capacity, repairer standby — before the first call. During the event, triage switches to event mode, loss reporting runs daily to claims and finance, and reinsurer notification obligations are tracked against treaty windows with the evidence attached.

Accumulation sits on the same foundation: per-event and aggregate exposure views stay current because exposure data quality — geocoding, secondary modifiers, valuation — is a monitored pipeline rather than an annual cleanup. The measurable deltas are first-contact time during events, surge cycle time, notification compliance and the confidence the capital and reinsurance functions can put in an accumulation number that used to be a quarterly estimate.

5. SIU referral and subrogation decisioning — the recovery ledger

The recovery build scores both ledgers from one platform. On the fraud side, referral scoring combines policy rules, anomaly models and network analytics — shared addresses, devices, repairers and claimant links across claims — so organised patterns surface as graphs rather than as repeated coincidences. Referrals arrive at the SIU workbench with the case pre-assembled: linked entities, claim history, prior referrals and the rationale for the referral itself, with the referral decision remaining a human one every time.

On the subrogation side, opportunity detection runs at intake and again at settlement: responsible third parties identified in the packet, product-defect patterns across claims, deductible recovery candidates — each scored, tracked and worked through demand assembly that generates the evidence pack the recovery or arbitration process needs. SIU outcomes feed back into thresholds so referral precision is managed as deliberately as catch rate. The measurable deltas are referral precision, recovery rate and days-to-demand, each reported from the recovery ledger the system maintains.

6. MGA and MGU programs, bordereaux operations — delegated authority, governed

The delegated-authority build governs the business you write through others. Submission intake from MGA and MGU portals is normalised against a schema registry, so E&S and program business enters the estate with its structure intact; bordereaux ingestion reconciles premium and loss against the delegated terms automatically, with quality scores per counterparty and exceptions routed to program-specific queues; and authority limits — binding authority, claim authority, exposure ceilings — are enforced as code rather than by trust.

Program oversight becomes telemetry instead of an annual review: program loss ratios, bordereaux quality trends and audit sampling by risk read from the same governed data, and TPA oversight files assemble from the evidence store the workflow already produces. The measurable deltas are bordereaux cycle time, reconciliation effort per program and the visibility underwriting leadership gains over delegated portfolios that previously reported in spreadsheets once a quarter.

The data plane: from vendor file drops to governed streams

Every decision gap in P&C traces back to a data gap, and the gaps here have a distinctive shape: half the decision inputs arrive as vendor files — estimates, cat footprints, telematics, bordereaux — and the estate treats them as attachments rather than as data. The data plane build inverts that. Claims events, policy transactions and premium flows land through change-data-capture into a governed lakehouse in near real time; vendor feeds land through the contract layer with schema validation, quality scoring and freshness SLAs, so an estimate, a footprint or a bordereau arrives as an owned, reconciled dataset.

Governance is engineered, not asserted. Every feed carries a schema contract with a named owner, a freshness SLA and a reconciliation check against the source system's own controls. The exposure-claims join — the asset pricing adequacy, cat accumulation and portfolio steering all depend on — is built once as a governed model rather than rebuilt every analysis, and every downstream consumer reads through the contract layer, so a schema change fails loudly at the contract instead of silently inside a rating model. The monthly reconciliation ritual becomes a monitored query with an owner.

  • Streaming claims and policy ingestion

    change-data-capture from adjudication, endorsements and premium events with exactly-once semantics, keeping triage and adequacy decisions current without touching finance batch

  • Vendor feed contracts

    estimatics, telematics, cat model and bordereaux feeds validated against schema contracts with quality scores, so vendor files stop being attachments

  • The governed exposure-claims join

    one model joining exposure, policy and claim data with lineage, reused by pricing, accumulation and reserving instead of rebuilt per analysis

  • Contract-enforced freshness

    per-feed SLAs with alerting and automated breach reports, so a late cat footprint pages an owner instead of silently skewing an accumulation view

The decision layer: triage, replay and reason codes

P&C runs on decisions with short clocks and long tails: a claim assignment made in minutes shapes the reserve for months, a rate quote carries conduct and fairness obligations, a SIU referral carries complaint risk. The decision layer we build treats those tails as first-class requirements. Every decision records its inputs, feature values, model versions, policy cutoffs and reason codes in a replay store that answers an unfair-claims question, a rate-challenge question or a consumer complaint as a query. When an examiner or a claimant asks why, the answer is retrieved from the record the decision made.

Champion/challenger governance is built in rather than bolted on: triage, pricing and referral challengers run on shadow traffic with performance tracked in the registry your model-risk function owns, promotions pass through the gates your framework already defines, and rollbacks are rehearsed rather than improvised. The same decision layer hosts claims triage, estimate review, pricing and adequacy, and SIU scoring, so the governance surface is one registry and one replay store — instead of four platforms with four audit stories and no shared explanation of the same claim.

The agentic layer: digital workers with audit trails

The backlog work in P&C — FNOL packet assembly, estimate pre-review, subrogation demand drafting, bordereaux matching, filing evidence collation — follows patterns an agentic workforce can absorb without touching settlement authority. Dataq Software's agent platform runs digital workers with explicit role scopes: the tools they may call, the systems they may read, the actions that require a human checkpoint and the budget of work per run. Every run logs its steps, tool calls and outputs to the same evidence store the human workflow uses, so an agent's file is auditable exactly like an adjuster's.

Where generative agents add leverage — summarising claim files for adjusters, drafting subrogation demands from the structured record, assembling filing justification drafts — the pattern is grounded generation with citation obligations and human authorship of record. Agents never settle claims, never change reserves, never file rates and never contact customers without review; they move the work to the decision point and stop. The capacity story is measured in cleared files per adjuster per day, which is how claims and underwriting leaders who sponsor these programs justify them upward.

Integration engineering: the estate stays, the contracts change

P&C estates are layered over decades and dominated by vendor platforms, and the fastest way to fail a program is to promise greenfield. Our integration posture is contractual: we define schema contracts with each system owner, land data through CDC and governed APIs, write back through the claims and policy interfaces your change process already controls, and keep every new component independently deployable behind feature flags. The claims core is not replaced; it is supplied with better context and drained of the triage and review work it was never good at.

The vendor chain gets the same treatment. Estimatics, cat model, telematics and data-enrichment vendors sit behind an abstraction layer with contract tests, so a vendor swap is a configuration change with a migration runbook rather than a re-platform — and the negotiating leverage that comes with it. Where a legacy admin or mainframe component must stay, the offload pattern applies: the batch cycle keeps posting, while the decision surfaces read from the stream the offload produces. This is unglamorous work, and it is the reason the builds above hold up in production instead of in a pilot.

  • Estimatics and cat vendor abstraction

    vendor signals behind one contract-tested interface so a vendor swap is configuration with a migration runbook, not surgery

  • Legacy and mainframe offload

    batch windows preserved, decisions fed from CDC streams, no change freeze negotiated to ship a model

How an engagement runs

The engagement shape is deliberately short on ceremony and long on measurement. It starts with the two-week diagnostic: one workflow baselined end to end, the leak quantified in your own telemetry, the data contracts inventoried, and a written feasibility read you can circulate — including the uncomfortable findings. If the delta is real, the build is scoped against the gates your change and model-risk processes define; if it is not, you keep the measurement design and the baseline either way.

  • Weeks 1-2: diagnostic and baseline

    workflow instrumentation, leak quantification, contract inventory, feasibility read with the delta estimate and the confounders named

  • Weeks 3-8: first vertical slice

    one lane live behind flags with replay and evidence running from day one — for claims that is one claim family; for pricing, one line of business

  • Weeks 9-16: gates, controls, expansion

    model-risk documentation landed, QC sampling live, remaining lanes added, capacity telemetry reporting to the operations review

  • Quarterly: challenger reviews

    triage, pricing and referral challengers evaluated in the registry, thresholds re-optimised, the baseline re-verified against the control group

Delivery runs in your delivery rhythm, not a parallel one: our engineers work in your repositories or a shared workspace with the same CI, the same security review and the same access model your teams use. Knowledge transfer is part of the gates, not a closing slide — by the expansion phase your engineers, adjusters and actuaries are operating the builds, because the runbooks, contracts and evaluation suites are theirs from week one.

The team and the operating rhythm

A P&C build team is small and senior: a lead engineer who has run decision systems in regulated carriers, data engineers who own the CDC and contract layer, ML engineers who own scoring and evaluation, a controls engineer who owns the evidence layer, and a domain lead who has run claims desks, pricing cycles or delegated-authority programs in production estates. Agentic-workflow specialists join for the file-assembly builds, and an actuarial SME sits close enough to the pricing build to keep the filing calendar honest. The team works to your release calendar and your gate vocabulary from the first week.

The rhythm is the same at every client: weekly operations review on the workflow's own telemetry, fortnightly gate submissions to change and model-risk, quarterly challenger reviews aligned to the filing calendar, and an incident practice that treats the replay store and the evidence layer as production-critical systems — because by the second quarter, they are. Escalations go to named engineers, not to a queue.

Failure modes we design against

Programs like these fail in known ways, and in P&C the failure modes carry a weather pattern: the catastrophe that arrives mid-program tests everything at once. We name them in the feasibility read and design against them explicitly, because a program that cannot survive its second quarter — or its first storm — is not worth starting.

  • Severity blindness

    triage that mis-scores severity routes complex claims to the wrong lane, and the cost surfaces weeks later as reserve strengthening — mis-assignment is a tracked first-class metric wired back to the triage features

  • Repairer friction

    estimate benchmarks that fight the network instead of informing it — scorecards ship with dispute processes and variance context, because a scorecard without due process leaks through behaviour instead

  • Filing evidence debt

    pricing models in production with justification reconstructed each filing — the registry generates the documentation during the build, or the pricing build is not done

  • Cat model brittleness

    vendor integrations that break exactly when the event arrives — contract-tested feeds with rehearsed fallbacks are a gate, and the fallback run is practiced against synthetic footprints

  • SIU over-referral

    referral volume rising while precision falls, landing on customers as complaints and on regulators as patterns — precision at fixed catch rate is managed in the same review as the recovery numbers

  • Pilot purgatory

    a demo built on extracted claims data that never integrates — we ship the first vertical slice through your change process, flags and gates included, within the first two months

Questions P&C leaders ask us

  • Do we need to replace our claims platform first?

    No. None of the six builds require a core replacement. They consume streams from the claims and policy estate plus contract-tested vendor feeds, and write back through your interfaces. The core stays; it stops being the bottleneck for decisions.

  • How does this survive rate review and model governance?

    Every pricing and triage component ships with its registry entry: lineage, evaluation results, fairness reviews, drift monitoring and decision replay. Filing justification generates from the same registry, so rate review reads artifacts the system already produces.

  • Will adjusters see their work replaced?

    The builds move packet assembly, estimate pre-review and evidence collation to agents, and put judgment back in adjuster hands with complete, cited files. Capacity is redeployed to complex claims; programs are justified in cleared files per adjuster, not headcount removed.

  • What happens during a catastrophe mid-program?

    The data plane and triage service are designed for event mode from the first release: footprints, exposure joins and surge telemetry are rehearsed against synthetic events before a real one tests them, and the fallback paths are documented runbooks, not improvisation.

  • How do you measure success without gaming?

    Baselines are locked during the diagnostic, controls or holdouts are used where experiments are cheap, and every metric reports from system telemetry rather than a status deck. The feasibility read names the confounders before anyone commits to a target.

  • What is the smallest program worth running?

    One workflow, one baseline, one vertical slice — FNOL triage and estimate review are the two most common starters because their clocks and variance telemetry are already instrumented. If the diagnostic cannot find the delta, we say so and stop.

  • Can you govern our MGA and TPA programs too?

    Yes — delegated authority is one of the six builds: normalised submission intake, reconciled bordereaux, authority limits enforced as code and program telemetry that replaces the annual review. The carrier finally sees the business it is on the hook for.

  • Who owns the build after you leave?

    Your teams, with the runbooks, contracts, evaluation suites and registry entries transferred as gate conditions during expansion — not as a closing deliverable. Several programs run entirely in-house after the second quarter.

Start with the factory that leaks most — FNOL triage, estimate review, pricing adequacy or cat response — and we will run the two-week diagnostic that quantifies the leak in your own claim telemetry. If the delta is real, the build is scoped against your gates; if it is not, you keep the baseline either way.
