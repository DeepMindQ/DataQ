Energy & Utilities

Energy and utilities is the one sector where the balance sheet, the physics and the regulator sit in the same room. A utility moves molecules and electrons on schedules it does not fully control, under market codes it did not write, through assets whose failure modes were learned the hard way. The operating day is a chain of decisions — dispatch, integrity, leakage, settlement — each one made on telemetry that arrives late, partial or unlabeled. Margin and license to operate both live in how fast those decisions get context. This page is the engineering picture of that decision machinery across the sector: the systems it runs on, the exact points where value leaks, and the six deep builds Dataq Software ships to close the gap.

How the sector's operating machinery actually runs

Across energy and utilities, one operating pattern repeats: a control room makes commitments in real time, a planning function makes promises for years ahead, and the two run on different data with no shared version of truth. The control room runs SCADA and DCS against physics — breaker states, valve positions, chlorine residuals, compressor and pump duty — and its decisions are only as good as the telemetry behind them. Planning runs asset registers, hydraulic models and capital portfolios on data that refreshes quarterly at best. Between them sits the historian, a PI-class store of every reading the estate produces, holding terabytes of context that almost no decision service ever queries. The ISA-95 layering meant to organize all this has in practice given each layer its own dialect of the truth.

The commercial machinery runs in parallel and on a different clock. Traders and schedulers take positions against day-ahead and intraday markets while the control room balances the system; settlement teams then reconcile scheduled volumes against measured flows, and the residue becomes imbalance cost, a curtailment claim or an invoice dispute that lingers for quarters. Field crews execute the physical decisions — a switching order, a dig request, a permit-to-work, a crew rerouted around a storm — through work-management and outage systems that rarely exchange context with the data estate. Customer-facing functions sit at the edge of the same pattern: meter data management, billing accuracy, hardship and conservation programs, each one regulated and judgment-bearing.

Layered over all of it is the compliance estate, and it is not decorative: NERC CIP for bulk-power cyber duties, IEC 62443 for OT zoning, NIS2 and DORA-class resilience obligations for essential entities, environmental permits with sampling schedules, methane measurement rules on the hydrocarbon side, CSRD-grade sustainability reporting with assurance attached. None of these regimes is satisfied by a document written once; each is satisfied by evidence the operating systems generate continuously, or fail to. The sector's structural problem is that evidence, decisions and physics live in three different stacks. Everything Dataq Software builds here exists to join those stacks at the decision points, without disturbing the physics.

Where the value leaks

Ask operations leaders across the sector where the money leaks and the answers converge on the same eight places, described in their own words. Dataq Software scores each of them in your estate before recommending a build, because the leak that dominates a control room is often minor in a treatment works, and the leak that dominates a wind fleet barely registers in a refinery.

  • Forecasts that miss where it costs most

    Renewable supply, demand elasticity and weather coupling have outgrown the legacy forecasting stack. Every miss becomes imbalance cost, curtailment left unclaimed or a flexibility dispatch made too late. The forecast error compounds through the whole chain: bidding, unit commitment, maintenance windows and settlement arguments all inherit it, and nobody can attribute the cost precisely enough to fix the worst contributors first.

  • Telemetry without context

    The historian holds every reading the estate produces, but tags arrive without units, asset ties or operating-mode context. Data scientists spend months reconstructing what a process engineer knows by heart, and decision services misfire on silent sensor drift. The instrumented estate is rich; the decision estate starves beside it, because context is undocumented rather than unavailable.

  • Assets maintained by calendar, not condition

    Transformers, turbines, pipelines and pumps hold failure precursors nobody sees because inspection schedules predate the sensors that could inform them. Condition-based maintenance is impossible without sensing joined to the asset register, and the failures that do occur are the unscheduled ones — the most expensive kind, on the assets that matter most.

  • Curtailment and imbalance accepted as weather

    When the market takes energy the operator cannot store or shift, the loss gets booked as an act of nature. But the record shows the same portfolios recovering double-digit percentages of that cost once curtailment analytics, storage co-optimization and faster scheduling decisions are in place. The difference between accepted and recovered is a decision loop, not a forecast.

  • Emissions assembled by estimate

    Methane, flare and sustainability numbers get built from emission factors and spreadsheet judgment, then defended to auditors and buyers who increasingly expect measurement. Methane MRV duties, CSRD assurance and customer scrutiny all move toward instrumented truth, so every year the gap between estimate and measurement becomes a commercial as well as a regulatory exposure.

  • OT estates flat and unmonitored

    IT and OT convergence, remote-access growth and legacy SCADA create attack paths with physical consequences, while IEC 62443 zoning and NERC CIP duties expand faster than programs mature. The evidence for audits is assembled by hand from systems that could produce it continuously, and nobody can say quickly what a compromised zone would have reached.

  • Field decisions starved of context

    Crew dispatch, permit-to-work and storm response run through work-management systems disconnected from network, asset and weather context. The technician who opens the wrong isolation or digs on the wrong main is rarely careless; they were starved of context their systems already hold. Every missed window compounds into overtime, penalties and complaints.

  • Unit economics nobody can state

    Cost per megawatt-hour delivered, per leak located, per contact resolved, per kilometre inspected — the numbers exist in fragments across finance spreadsheets and operational systems. Without per-asset, per-workflow cost telemetry, capital allocation and platform decisions get made on invoice totals and habit, and the biggest efficiency lever in the sector stays unpulled.

The pattern across all eight leaks: the physics is instrumented but the decisions are not. Every leak is a context problem before it is a modeling problem, which is why Dataq Software's first build in this sector is almost always the operations data fabric — not a smarter model sitting on top of a broken decision supply chain.

The deep engineering catalog for energy and utilities

The catalog below is the whole of our sector practice. Each build is a named engineering service with defined data flows, decision services, guardrails and integration points — deployed against your estate in weeks, not quarters, and designed so the evidence regulators want is a by-product of running the system.

1. The operations data fabric — historian to lakehouse with context attached

The build joins the systems that already hold the truth: historian tags at native cadence, SCADA events, AMI head-end reads, CMMS work orders, asset registers, lab results and meteorological feeds. A governed stream bus moves them into an operations lakehouse without disturbing the control path, and every feed lands with an asset-model spine attached — the equipment hierarchy, operating modes and units that make a tag answerable. Store-and-forward gaps from remote sites are surfaced rather than papered over, because a decision service that trusts gappy telemetry is worse than one that refuses.

Decision services: a single queryable truth for integrity, forecasting, settlement and compliance; feature stores shared by every downstream model so two teams never compute the same signal differently; freshness SLAs per feed with named owners. Guardrails: read-first integration everywhere, schema contracts on every stream, quality gates that page a human instead of skewing a model. Integration points: PI-class historians, OPC servers, AMI head-ends, CMMS, LIMS, weather providers. The fabric is the substrate every other build in this catalog stands on, which is why it ships first.

2. Forecasting and scheduling decision hubs

The build replaces spreadsheet forecasting with a weather-coupled decision hub: renewable generation and demand predicted at asset and portfolio level, confidence intervals attached, and the forecast wired directly into the schedules that consume it — unit commitment, maintenance windows, bidding curves, storage dispatch. Backcasting runs continuously against locked baselines so forecast quality is a league table by zone and product, not an anecdote. Solver tournaments evaluate scheduling formulations in parallel, because the formulation that wins at one grid topology loses at another and nobody should guess.

Decision services: bid recommendations with risk bounds, imbalance-exposure dashboards that name the contributing feeders or farms, maintenance-window proposals that respect both physics and market opportunity. Guardrails: forecasts degrade gracefully with data gaps and say so; every schedule carries its inputs for replay; the operator or trader approves before anything executes. Integration points: EMS and scheduling systems, market interfaces, weather providers, DERMS. This is the build behind the published 31-percent forecast-error reduction across three European control zones.

3. Asset performance and integrity analytics

The build turns the instrumented estate into a condition ledger: transformer dissolved-gas analysis, breaker operations, turbine vibration and CMS channels, pump curves, pipeline corrosion circuits, mains break history — joined to the asset register and the CMMS so every reading lands on the equipment that produced it. Failure-mode models run per asset class with validated precursors, and risk-based inspection plans start consuming current condition instead of last inspection date. The output is a ranked intervention list with consequences quantified, not a heatmap nobody can act on.

Decision services: integrity operating windows computed from live conditions, dig and inspection worklists ranked by risked consequence, capital-renewal prioritization with failure cost modeled honestly. Guardrails: recommendations never bypass engineering approval; every computed rate carries its inputs for replay; sensor coverage below threshold blocks the calculation rather than faking it. Integration points: CMMS, historians, ILI vendors, SCADA, engineering document registries. This is the build that makes condition-based maintenance real on fleets that were maintained by calendar for forty years.

4. OT segmentation and cyber-resilience engineering

The build treats cyber events as operational events. IEC 62443 zone-and-conduit models are designed against your actual network, then enforced and monitored: OT traffic baselines established per zone, drift alerted, remote access brokered through identity with sessions recorded. NERC CIP evidence — access reviews, change records, monitoring logs — is generated continuously from the systems of record instead of assembled by a team every audit cycle. Recovery is rehearsed like a turnaround: runbooks, restoration sequencing and tabletop exercises with the control room, evidence attached.

Decision services: zone health dashboards, access-risk reviews prioritized by consequence, incident playbooks tied to OT scenarios, third-party register maintenance. Guardrails: no change enters a controlled system outside MOC; segmentation designs are validated with your OT engineers before deployment; monitoring is passive-first so the control path stays untouched. Integration points: SCADA networks, historians, identity providers, GRC tooling. NIS2 and DORA-class duties land on the same evidence pipeline, because the artifacts those regimes want are the ones a healthy OT estate produces anyway.

5. Emissions MRV and certificate provenance

The build computes emissions from measurements instead of factors wherever instruments exist, and labels estimates honestly where they do not. Flare flows, methane detection surveys, tank measurements and fuel consumption fuse into site-level accounts with uncertainty ranges, reconciled monthly and traceable from any reported figure back to the reading that produced it. On the certificate side, guarantees-of-origin and RECs reconcile against generation telemetry with provenance anchored per certificate, so double-counting risk and buyer scrutiny both meet the same auditable record.

Decision services: intensity per unit of output, leak-priority worklists, flare-event root-cause context, certificate inventory with settlement-ready evidence. Guardrails: uncertainty published with every number; estimates flagged by provenance class; lineage unbroken from sensor to disclosure. Integration points: SCADA and flow metering, OGI survey providers, GO and REC registries, CSRD reporting pipelines. The compliance payoff is audit as a query; the commercial payoff is that verified low-carbon claims stop being a discount and start being a price premium.

6. Field, outage and permit orchestration

The build closes the loop between decisions and the crews who execute them. Outage management, crew dispatch, permit-to-work and storm response are orchestrated against live network, asset and weather context: restoration sequencing optimized for customers-at-risk, permit packages assembled with isolation evidence attached, technician routing built from skills, assets and access windows. Every executed step writes back — as-built notes, photos, test results — so the estate's records heal instead of drifting further from field reality.

Decision services: storm-mode crew allocation, SAIDI- and MAIDI-aware prioritization, permit queues sequenced by operational criticality, vegetation and maintenance routing that respects weather windows. Guardrails: safety-critical steps stay human-gated by design; every switching order carries its network model snapshot; permit evidence is immutable once signed. Integration points: OMS, ADMS, CMMS, mobile workforce platforms, GIS. The published water-utility program that cut non-revenue water 21 percent ran on this pattern — district metering data closing a decision loop that ended at the field crew.

Sub-industry programs: what differs on the ground

The six builds above are sector-wide; what changes below is the ground truth. Each sub-industry runs a different physics, a different regulatory spine and a different decision frontier. The hub build ports; the workflows, systems and regulators named here define what the local program must survive.

  • Oil and gas: integrity, turnarounds and the methane clock

    Workflows concentrate on mechanical integrity, turnaround execution and permit-to-work under process-safety regimes such as Seveso and OSHA PSM. Systems run DCS, SCADA, PI-class historians, WITSML drilling feeds and ILI defect records. Regulators read the safety case, methane measurement duties and pipeline-integrity clocks. The decision frontier is joining corrosion evidence to planning and scheduling — margin leaks in the seams between inspection, operations and the commercial loop.

  • Power grid: real-time physics under market and CIP obligations

    Control rooms run EMS, ADMS and SCADA over IEC 61850 and DNP3, with synchrophasors per IEEE C37.118 feeding state estimation. Workflows concentrate on forecasting, switching, outage restoration, DER flexibility and storm response. NERC CIP and market codes bind the evidence. The frontier is decision speed: imbalance, curtailment and restoration sequencing decided on telemetry minutes old, not spreadsheets hours old.

  • Renewables: fleet performance and revenue under weather risk

    The asset is weather-exposed and the revenue is contractual: turbine SCADA with CMS vibration channels, inverter strings, soiling and IV-curve tracing feed availability and lost-production-factor waterfalls. Workflows concentrate on forecasting, portfolio bidding, curtailment recovery, O&M weather windows and warranty claims against OEMs. Grid codes and GO or REC registries bind the evidence; PPA settlement turns physics into cash. The frontier is separating underperformance from weather, honestly.

  • Water: leakage, quality and capital renewal under public duty

    Operations run treatment SCADA, district metered areas, pressure logging and chlorine-residual sensing on networks with century-old segments. Workflows concentrate on non-revenue water, burst prediction, regulatory sampling schedules, sewer overflow evidence and capital renewal prioritization. Drinking-water directives, environmental permits and NIS2 bind the duty. The frontier is the water balance: every decision loop from meter to main-break history closed within the month, not the audit.

The data plane: governed streams, not overnight dumps

Every decision gap in this sector traces back to a data gap, and most of those gaps are engineering choices rather than physics. Control systems hold the highest-frequency truth but sit behind read-only barriers; commercial systems hold the money view but update on batch cycles; field systems hold the as-built reality but speak in free text and photographs. The data plane's job is to make those three views queryable together at decision speed — with schema contracts on every feed, freshness SLAs with named owners, and quality gates that refuse silently-drifting sensors instead of letting them skew a model.

Governance is engineered, not asserted. Every value in the lakehouse can name its source system, its transformation chain and its quality state at read time, which is what makes the evidence layer possible later. Store-and-forward telemetry from remote wells, rural feeders and buried loggers arrives with gap metadata attached, so models can degrade honestly. Weather, market prices and regulatory calendars join as first-class feeds, because the decisions this sector makes are always conditional on all three.

  • Native-cadence historian ingestion

    OPC and PI interfaces stream tags at their native rate with compression semantics preserved, backfill handled explicitly, and the control path never touched — the historian keeps its role, and the lakehouse adds context to what it already holds

  • The asset-model spine

    equipment hierarchies tying every tag, work order and reading to the well, circuit, main or turbine that produced it, signed off by the engineers who own those hierarchies — context is a contract, not a convention

  • Quality gates with named owners

    staleness, flatlines and sensor drift detected at ingest; the affected feature set quarantined and the owner paged, so a stuck transmitter pages a technician instead of quietly degrading a forecast

  • Field-truth writeback

    as-built notes, photos and test results from the workbench flow back into the asset record with chain of custody, so the estate's documentation heals with every job instead of drifting further from reality

The decision layer: bounded optimization with replay

This sector runs on decisions with physical and regulatory tails: a commitment made to a market, a dig deferred on a pipeline, a permit granted for a window. The decision layer treats every one of them as a first-class object — inputs, model version, constraints active, expected delta, human approval — stored in a replay service that can re-derive any decision months later for an auditor, a regulator or an engineer asking what the system knew at the time. Champion and challenger run permanently: every forecasting method, scheduling formulation and inspection prioritization competes against the incumbent on locked baselines before it earns traffic.

The guardrails are as engineered as the models. Recommendations are bounded by validated operating envelopes, and anything approaching a boundary escalates with its reasoning attached. Decision rights are explicit — what the system may do alone, what it drafts for approval, what stays human by regulation or choice. Uncertainty is carried forward rather than averaged away: a forecast without its confidence interval, an integrity rate without its sensor-coverage caveat, do not reach a decision surface. The result is optimization the control room and the regulator can both interrogate.

The agentic layer: digital workers on the paperwork frontier

The backlog work in energy and utilities is evidentiary: assembling dig packs, drafting permit packages, reconciling settlement statements, reconstructing incident timelines, preparing MRV disclosures. These are retrieval, assembly and cross-checking jobs with clear success criteria — ideal for governed agents that work inside the platform with role scopes, tool allow-lists and complete run logs. Each agent ships with an evaluation suite: golden cases where the right answer is known, adversarial cases where the tempting answer is wrong, and a human checkpoint at every boundary that matters.

Where generative capability adds leverage — summarizing shift logs, drafting narrative sections of regulatory reports, translating engineering documents into structured records — it operates with retrieval grounding against your estate and provenance stamps on every claim. No agent issues a permit, changes a setpoint or submits a market bid; those boundaries are architectural, not aspirational. The agentic layer's output is reviewed work moving faster, and the audit trail to prove it — which is the difference between automation that survives an inspection and automation that becomes the finding.

Integration engineering: the estate stays, the contracts change

Sector estates are layered over decades — SCADA from three vendors, historians acquired with plants, metering systems inherited through consolidation — and the fastest way to stall a program is to treat that as debt to repay before starting. Dataq Software wraps the estate instead: adapters at native cadence, normalizers for vendor formats, schema contracts that make every upstream system answerable for freshness and shape. Control systems are read-first by policy; nothing writes back into a controlled system without your MOC process, and most builds never need to.

The vendor chain gets the same treatment. Historian exports, ILI vendors, OGI survey providers, weather services, meter-data processors and registry operators all become contract parties to the data plane: formats specified, freshness SLAs enforced, test harnesses run per delivery. When a vendor changes a format, the contract test fails before the decision layer notices — which is the difference between an integration estate and an integration apology.

  • Read-first control integration

    every path into SCADA, DCS or protection equipment starts read-only, at native cadence, with write access designed only where a workflow demands it and executed through your change process with your engineers in the loop

  • Vendor-format normalizers

    ILI geometry records, OGI surveys, lab results and registry files normalized at ingest with schema contracts, so vendor diversity never reaches a decision service

  • Meter-to-bill integrity joins

    AMI head-end, MDM validation rules and billing systems joined per meter point, so settlement and revenue disputes resolve against a shared record rather than dueling exports

  • Contract-tested weather and market feeds

    forecast providers, price curves and registry interfaces behind freshness SLAs and golden-day replay tests, because a stale weather feed is a silent forecast killer

How an engagement runs

The engagement shape is deliberately short on ceremony and long on measurement. It opens with a two-week diagnostic on the workflow you nominate — not a maturity assessment of the whole estate — and the diagnostic's product is a baseline in your own telemetry, a named delta with the confounders listed, and a scoped proposal only if the delta justifies the build. You keep the measurement design either way.

  • Weeks 1-2: diagnostic and baseline

    workflow instrumentation, leak quantification, contract inventory, feasibility read with the delta estimate and the confounders named in writing

  • Weeks 3-8: first vertical slice

    one lane of the workflow live behind flags with replay and evidence running from day one — one control zone, one circuit, one district metered area

  • Weeks 9-16: gates, controls, expansion

    controls landed, quality gates live, remaining lanes added, capacity and decision telemetry reporting to the operations review

  • Quarterly: challenger reviews

    forecast methods, scheduling formulations and prioritization models re-evaluated in the registry, baselines re-verified, the roadmap re-priced against measured deltas

Delivery runs inside your operating rhythm, not a parallel program tower: our engineers sit with the control room's stakeholders, the integrity team and the settlement desk, work through your change process for anything that touches a governed system, and hand over runbooks, evaluation suites and registry entries as gate conditions during expansion — not as a closing document after the invoice.

Failure modes we design against

Programs in this sector fail in known ways, and most of the failure modes are organizational wearing a technical costume. We name them here because prevention is a design decision, not a cultural aspiration.

  • Pilot purgatory

    a demo built on extracted data that never integrates — prevented by shipping the first vertical slice through your change process, flags and gates included, within the first two months

  • Context debt

    a lakehouse full of tags nobody can answer for because units, modes and asset ties were never contractual — the asset-model spine is a gate, not an optimization

  • Optimizer meets the envelope wall

    a scheduling or dispatch recommendation outside the validated operating envelope — bounded envelopes, uncertainty labels and engineer gates are architectural, not advisory

  • Evidence debt

    audit preparation consuming a team every cycle because artifacts were manual — the evidence layer generates them continuously or the build is not done

  • Control-room rejection

    a dashboard the operators ignore because it contradicts the alarm philosophy or hides its provenance — operator co-design and decision replay are how trust gets built

  • Free-text asset history

    analytics starved by work orders nobody can parse — taxonomy repair is scoped into the first quarter or the integrity build is declined

Questions energy and utilities leaders ask us

  • Do we need to replace our historian or EMS first?

    No. The builds consume streams from the systems you run and write back through governed interfaces. The estate stays; it stops being the bottleneck for decisions. Replacement conversations, where they happen later, happen with better evidence.

  • How do you work across the IT and OT boundary?

    Read-first by policy, zones per IEC 62443, identity-brokered remote access, and nothing entering a controlled system outside your management-of-change process. Our engineers work with your OT security team as peers, not as an exception to them.

  • Will this interfere with control-room operations?

    The control path is never a data source for writes; monitoring is passive-first. Recommendations surface in operator workflows with provenance and confidence, and the control room keeps final authority on anything physical.

  • Can you work inside our regulated procurement model?

    Yes. Deliverables are structured as scoped increments with defined evidence, which suits audit and procurement better than a monolith. Baselines and measurement designs are yours to keep, even if the program stops at the diagnostic.

  • What does the first quarter actually deliver?

    A baseline, one vertical slice live behind flags, the evidence layer running, and a feasibility read that names the confounders. If the delta is not real in your telemetry, the program stops there by design.

  • How do you measure success without gaming?

    Baselines locked during the diagnostic, control groups where operations allow, and every metric reported from system telemetry. The feasibility read states the confounders in writing before anyone commits to a target.

  • Who owns the models and evidence after you leave?

    Your teams, with runbooks, evaluation suites, model documentation and the replay store transferred as gate conditions during expansion. Several sector programs run entirely in-house after the second quarter.

  • Where should we start?

    Wherever the metric is cleanest and the baseline is cheapest: forecasting where imbalance is billed, integrity where inspection backlogs queue, non-revenue water where district metering exists. The diagnostic tells you which, with numbers.
