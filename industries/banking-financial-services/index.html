Banking & Financial Services

A banking group is several businesses sharing one balance sheet: a retail bank opening accounts and moving payments, a corporate bank underwriting credit, a markets arm executing orders, a payments franchise clearing other people's transactions, and a wealth business holding other people's assets. Beneath them sit shared utilities — KYC, screening, treasury, finance and risk reporting — and one supervisory perimeter that treats all of it as systemically important. The margin is decided in the machinery underneath: how fast a KYC file assembles, how precisely a fraud score cuts, how completely an audit artifact generates itself. This page is the sector-wide engineering picture: the systems the estate runs on, where money and control leak, the deep builds Dataq Software ships, and what moves on the P&L when they land.

The sector operating picture

Across banking and financial services, the day starts in several places at once. The retail book posts overnight batches and opens its channels. The corporate bank reviews drawdown notices and covenant reports. Markets desks connect to venues over FIX and check overnight exposures against limits. The payments franchise clears yesterday's scheme files and starts today's authorization stream. Each business keeps its own systems of record — the core, the loan servicing platform, the OMS/EMS, the issuer and acquirer stacks, the custody ledger — and each was bought, built or inherited on a different decade's assumptions.

The shared utilities carry the load nobody owns end to end. KYC and screening serve every business but run in their own case manager. Treasury funds them all from one liquidity book. Finance and risk pull into warehouses that reconcile to the ledger by monthly ritual. Messaging gateways — SWIFT, ISO 20022, FIX — move the money and the orders, and their translation layers quietly absorb whatever the source systems got wrong. When a supervisor asks a lineage question, the answer crosses five teams and two acquisitions.

The pressure on this machinery is structural, not cyclical. Instant rails compress decision windows from hours to seconds. DORA makes third-party ICT risk and incident evidence a board-level obligation. Basel-style capital regimes price model quality directly into RWA. The EU AI Act pulls credit scoring and risk models into a documented, high-risk inventory. And generative AI waits at every desk, useful and ungoverned, until someone designs the guardrails. The institutions pulling ahead rebuild the machinery — governed data, decision hubs with replay, agents with audit trails — instead of stacking another layer of dashboards on it.

The estate speaks a small set of standards — ISO 20022 and SWIFT for messaging, ISO 8583 for card transactions, FIX for orders, UCP 600 for documentary trade — and it answers to a dense set of obligations. Where the margin and control leak

Ask group operating-committee members where money and control leak and the answers converge on the same eight places. The mix differs by institution — a payments-led group leaks differently from a wealth-led one — but each leak is a data and decision engineering problem before it is a policy problem. Dataq Software's diagnostic scores all eight in your estate before recommending a build, and the feasibility read names which two or three dominate, because funding all eight at once is how programs stall.

  • KYC refresh cycles that queue instead of triage

    Due diligence refreshes are scheduled by risk rating but executed by analyst availability. File assembly across registries, screening vendors and core records is manual, so the risk logic that says review now is overruled by the queue that says wait. Regulators read queue depth as a risk signal, and remediation programs consume exactly the analysts they were meant to free.

  • Risk and finance estates that disagree

    Credit, market, liquidity and operational risk live in separate marts with divergent reference data, so the same exposure carries three values depending on which report answers. Accuracy and lineage expectations in the BCBS 239 style turn every audit into archaeology, and regulatory reporting leans on manual workarounds nobody can defend under questioning.

  • Generative AI stuck between pilot and policy

    Assistants for credit memos, research summaries and client drafts stall in model-risk review because guardrails, provenance and evaluation were not designed in. Shadow use fills the gap, creating the exposure the review was meant to prevent. The fix is platform-level: permission-aware retrieval, citation obligations and evaluation suites agreed with the second line before the first prompt ships.

  • Fraud decisions tuned for a slower world

    Batch scoring and static rules were built for card-present economics. Instant rails move the decision into the authorization path, measured in seconds, and social-engineering fraud on faster payments pays out before anyone phones the bank. The defense has to be event-native — device, behavior, network and graph signals scored inside the switch timeout.

  • Resilience evidence assembled by spreadsheet

    DORA-class regimes demand registers of ICT third parties, tested incident playbooks, recovery evidence and exit strategies. In most estates the evidence lives in mailboxes and quarter-end scrambles. Every assessment becomes archaeology, and the first serious supervisory question about a critical vendor takes three weeks to answer.

  • Revenue leakage across fees, interchange and billing

    Wealth fee schedules, corporate commitment and agency fees, interchange and scheme billings are computed by systems that rarely reconcile to the contract. Recoveries happen when a client complains. Billing-to-ledger reconciliation is manual or approximate, so the leakage is structural and invisible at group level.

  • Model sprawl without a shared decision layer

    Fraud, credit, AML and marketing each run their own scoring stack with their own feature definitions. Velocity means three things to three teams. Champion/challenger exists in one stack and not the others, and adverse-action reason codes are reconstructed by hand when the regulator asks. The missing asset is a shared decision layer, not more models.

  • Unit economics nobody can state

    Cost per payment processed, per account opened, per contact resolved, per break cleared — the numbers exist as fragments in finance spreadsheets. Without per-workflow cost telemetry, platform consolidation and cloud spend are decided on invoice totals, and transformation business cases argue from averages nobody trusts.

The pattern across all eight leaks: decisions are made without their context, and evidence is produced after the fact by hand. Context is a data engineering problem and evidence is a systems problem — which is why our first build in this sector is almost always the decision lakehouse and the evidence layer, not a smarter model.

How Dataq Software works banking and financial services

Dataq Software works with retail banks, corporate and investment banks, payment institutions and capital-markets firms as an engineering partner that ships into production. We do not arrive with a replacement core, a rip-and-replace migration story, or a strategy deck that ends where the hard part begins. The engagement model is a two-week diagnostic on one workflow — a leak baselined in your own telemetry — followed by a scoped build that ships behind your change process, your model-risk framework and the third-party registers your resilience regime requires.

Three engineering commitments shape everything we deliver in the sector. First, every automated decision carries replay: inputs, feature values, model versions, policy cutoffs and reason codes are stored so an adverse-action or best-execution question twelve months later is a query, not an investigation. Second, every generative component is permission-aware and citation-bound — assistants answer from your knowledge bases, product terms and case records, not the open internet, and every answer shows its sources. Third, every build lands with its evidence layer: the artifacts a reviewer will ask for are generated by the system, not assembled by a team the week before the review.

The published record behind this posture: a top-10 European retail bank cut fraud losses 38% on instant rails while raising approval rates; a multinational corporate bank cleared its KYC refresh backlog 70% faster with agentic file assembly; and a tier-1 US bank moved to automated third-party and incident evidence under a DORA-grade posture. Where the metric is clean we contract against the delta; where it is political or confounded we say so in the feasibility read and scope fixed-fee instead.

The deep engineering catalog for banking and financial services

The catalog below is the whole of our banking and financial services practice. Each build is named for the workflow it changes, sized in the diagnostic, and shipped with the controls the workflow's regulator expects. None require replacing a core; all require taking data contracts seriously. The sub-industry pages name how each build lands differently on the ground, with the systems, clocks and metrics specific to each.

1. The decision lakehouse for risk, finance and client data

The build consolidates what the sector fragments. Change-data-capture from cores, switches, trading platforms, loan servicing, custody and case managers lands into a governed lakehouse through schema contracts with named owners and freshness SLAs. A conformed client and counterparty master applies survivorship rules your MDM owners sign; an entity graph links customers, corporates, ultimate beneficial owners and related parties so the same relationship means the same thing to KYC, credit and AML.

Regulatory reporting reads through the same contract layer: capital inputs, liquidity ratios, transaction reporting and ECL staging pull from certified datasets with lineage the second line can walk. The overnight batch keeps running for finance and reconciliation, but decision surfaces stop depending on it. This build lands first in sector programs because every other build — fraud scoring, limit checks, advisor copilots, agentic file assembly — reads from it and inherits its lineage.

2. Real-time decision hub for payment and trading events

Instant rails and electronic markets removed the batch from the front line, so the decision layer has to be event-native. The hub consumes authorization streams, payment messages and order-flow events, computes features in milliseconds in a shared feature store, and evaluates policy plus models in one decision service that returns approve, decline, step-up or route inside the timeout the channel imposes. Stand-in and degrade paths are modeled explicitly so offline decisions do not bypass scoring silently.

Champion/challenger governance runs in the same registry the model-risk function owns: challengers see shadow traffic, promotions pass your gates, rollbacks are rehearsed. Every decision records inputs, feature values, model versions, policy cutoffs and reason codes into the replay store, so a fraud decline, a limit breach or a suitability gate can be explained months later as a query. One registry, one replay store, one set of gates — across fraud, credit, limits and next-best-action.

3. Agentic file assembly for KYC, credit and dispute files

The back book of this sector is paperwork: KYC refresh files, credit memos and covenant packages, dispute evidence, corporate-action notices and break documentation. The agentic build runs digital workers with explicit role scopes — which tools they may call, which systems they may read, which actions require a human checkpoint and what work one run may consume. Agents assemble files from registries, screening results, financials, transaction logs and prior correspondence, then hand over at the decision point.

Generative components are grounded and citation-bound: summaries reference the case record, drafts keep a human author of record, and every run logs its steps to the same evidence store the human workflow uses. The published record includes a multinational corporate bank that cleared its KYC refresh backlog 70% faster with this pattern; the same pattern clears dispute evidence packs and covenant packages in retail and wealth estates. Capacity is measured in cleared cases per analyst per day.

4. Grounded assistants for servicing, advisory and credit desks

Every desk in this sector loses hours to context assembly: the contact-center agent swivel-chairs across six screens, the advisor rebuilds portfolio context from terminals and PDFs, the credit officer re-types financials from filings. The assistant build is retrieval-grounded, deliberately not open-ended: it answers from a permission-aware index of product terms, knowledge bases, research, client records and case files, with citations on every answer and transactional authority handed to governed APIs or to humans by policy.

The evaluation harness ships with the build: golden question sets per intent, containment measured against post-contact satisfaction rather than deflection alone, escalation-context quality scored on what the human receives. Model-risk documentation — data lineage, evaluation results, guardrail inventory, drift monitoring — is part of delivery, so the second-line review is a walkthrough of artifacts the system already produces. Voice and chat channels share one retrieval core so grounding does not decay at the channel boundary.

5. The regulatory evidence and resilience layer

Supervisory questions in this sector are evidence questions: show the third-party register, the incident playbook, the recovery test, the model documentation, the decision trail. The build generates those artifacts continuously instead of assembling them before a review. ICT third-party entries, criticality assessments and exit notes attach to every vendor integration we touch; incident playbooks and recovery tests execute and log per release; the model-risk registry carries evaluation results, drift monitoring and guardrail inventories for every AI component.

The tier-1 US bank in our published record moved third-party and incident evidence to automated collection under a DORA-grade posture, and the pattern generalizes: the evidence layer treats audit as a query, not a quarter of archaeology. Compliance teams keep their frameworks; what changes is that the artifacts exist before they are asked for, with lineage to the systems that produced them. Reviews shrink from expeditions to walkthroughs.

6. Constrained optimization for rebalancing, collections and collateral

Three workflows in this sector are solver problems wearing policy constraints: portfolio rebalancing against tax lots and mandate rules, collections treatment against conduct and vulnerability constraints, and collateral optimization against eligibility and haircut schedules. The build formulates each as a constrained optimization with the constraints encoded as hard rules — mandate restrictions, quiet hours and consent state, eligibility criteria — and the objective functions tuned against the metric the business actually owns.

Solver tournaments benchmark candidate formulations on your book — drift-to-trade, cure rate, collateral cost — with champion/challenger governance and decision logs that record which candidate won and why. Treatment and rebalancing decisions replay with their inputs, so a client or an examiner can reconstruct why this portfolio traded, why this customer received this treatment, why this collateral was called. The economics report from held-out controls, because these are among the few places in banking where a clean experiment is cheap.

Sub-industry programs: what differs on the ground

The sector builds share one spine, but the ground differs by sub-industry — different workflows, different systems of record, different regulators asking different questions. The differences change how a build is scoped, which estate it integrates against, and which metric justifies it; the four bullets below are the short version, and each sub-industry page carries the deep picture, the estate table and the builds as they land there.

  • Retail and consumer banking: conduct at volume

    The workflow is account opening, payments, servicing, disputes and collections at consumer volume. The systems are the core, the card switch, origination and the contact center. The regulators write conduct rules into every interaction — dispute clocks, vulnerability handling, advertising standards — so builds carry reason codes, timers and channel constraints as first-class requirements. Fraud defense and cost per account dominate the P&L.

  • Corporate and investment banking: judgment under credit discipline

    The workflow is origination, credit approval, syndication, trade finance and servicing on bespoke deals. The systems are loan origination and servicing, the SWIFT corporate channel, collateral and limits platforms. The regulators are prudential — capital, large exposures, IFRS 9 staging — plus AML regimes that treat corporate structures and UBO chains as the primary risk. Documents are the data; covenants and memos are the decisions.

  • Payments and cards: scheme economics at authorization speed

    The workflow is authorization, clearing, settlement, disputes and merchant risk under scheme rulebooks. The systems are issuer and acquirer stacks, instant rails gateways, token vaults and dispute platforms. The regulators are hybrid: scheme rules with real financial teeth, PCI DSS 4.0, PSD2/PSD3 and instant-payments regulation. Milliseconds and basis points decide the P&L simultaneously, and both are engineering quantities.

  • Capital markets and wealth: fiduciary duty

    The workflow is mandate onboarding, portfolio construction, execution, custody, corporate actions, fee billing and client reporting. The systems are portfolio management, OMS/EMS over FIX, custody and fund services, billing engines. The regulators are fiduciary: suitability and best execution, client-asset protection, performance and marketing standards. Breaks and billing errors are the margin story; suitability evidence is the license story.

The data plane: from overnight batches to governed streams

Every decision gap in the sector traces to a data gap, and most data gaps trace to the same architecture habit: systems of record write to files, files load into marts overnight, and decisions run on whatever the last load knew. The data plane build inverts the pattern where it matters. Authorization streams, FIX drops, SWIFT messages, core postings and case events land through change-data-capture into a governed lakehouse in near real time. The overnight batch keeps running for finance and reconciliation, but decision surfaces stop depending on it.

Governance is engineered, not asserted. Every feed carries a schema contract with a named owner, a freshness SLA and a reconciliation check against the source system's own controls. The conformed entity master applies survivorship rules agreed with your data owners, and every downstream consumer — fraud features, limit checks, regulatory extracts, advisor and agent desktops — reads through the contract layer, so a schema change fails loudly at the contract instead of silently in a model. The reconciliation ritual that ate the first week of every month becomes a monitored query.

  • Streaming ingestion across the front line

    change-data-capture from cores, switches, OMS/EMS, loan servicing and custody with exactly-once semantics into the lakehouse, keeping decision surfaces current without touching batch finance

  • Contract-enforced freshness

    per-feed SLAs with alerting and automated breach reports, so a late settlement-file load pages an owner instead of silently skewing features

  • Conformed client and counterparty master

    survivorship and source-precedence rules signed off by data owners, with the duplicate-suspect queue surfaced rather than hidden

  • Feature store discipline

    one definition of velocity, exposure and contact recency shared by fraud, credit and marketing — the end of two teams computing the same feature differently

The decision layer: scoring, replay and reason codes

Banking runs on decisions with legal tails: a credit decline carries adverse-action obligations, a fraud decline carries customer-experience obligations, a limit breach carries escalation obligations, a suitability gate carries fiduciary ones. The decision layer we build treats those tails as first-class requirements. Every decision records its inputs, feature values, model versions, policy rules and the reason codes the obligation requires, in a replay store that answers audit questions as queries. When a customer, a client or an examiner asks why, the answer is retrieved, not reconstructed.

Champion/challenger governance is built in rather than bolted on: challengers run on shadow traffic with performance tracked in the registry the model-risk function owns, promotions pass the gates your SR 11-7-aligned framework already defines, and rollbacks are rehearsed rather than improvised. The same decision service layer hosts fraud scoring, credit and limit checks, next-best-action and treatment assignment, so the governance surface is one registry, one replay store, one set of gates — instead of five platforms with five audit stories.

The agentic layer: digital workers with audit trails

The backlog work of banking — file assembly, evidence collection, screening preparation, reconciliation matching, break clearance — follows patterns an agentic workforce can absorb without touching judgment authority. Dataq Software's agent platform runs digital workers with explicit role scopes: the tools they may call, the systems they may read, the actions that require a human checkpoint and the budget of work per run. Every run logs its steps, tool calls and outputs to the same evidence store the human workflow uses, so an agent's file is auditable exactly like an analyst's.

Where generative agents add leverage — summarizing case files, drafting KYC refresh summaries, assembling dispute evidence packs, preparing committee materials — the pattern is grounded generation with citation obligations and human authorship of record. Agents never post to the core, never send customer or client communications without review, and never change a screening or credit decision; they move the work to the decision point and stop. The capacity story is measured in cleared cases per analyst per day, which is how operations leaders justify these programs upward.

Dataq Software's answer is the evidence layer — the artifacts a reviewer will ask for are generated continuously by the systems themselves. Integration engineering: the estate stays, the contracts change

Banking estates are layered over decades, and the fastest way to fail a program is to promise greenfield. Our integration posture is contractual: we define schema contracts with each system owner, land data through CDC and governed APIs, write back through the posting interfaces, message gateways and workflow hooks your change process already controls, and keep every new component independently deployable behind feature flags. Cores are not replaced; they are supplied with better context and drained of the decision work they were never good at.

The vendor and message chains get the same treatment. IDV, screening, bureau, market-data and device-intelligence vendors sit behind an abstraction layer with contract tests, so a vendor swap is a configuration change with a migration runbook rather than a re-platform. SWIFT and ISO 20022 migration becomes an opportunity to enforce contracts at the gateway instead of another translation patch. Where a mainframe component must stay, the offload pattern applies: the batch window keeps posting while decision surfaces read from the stream it produces.

  • Write-back through your interfaces

    credits, status changes, treatment updates and payments flow through the core, servicing and gateway interfaces your change process already governs

  • Vendor abstraction with contract tests

    IDV, screening, bureau, market-data and device signals behind one interface, so a vendor swap is configuration, not surgery

  • Messaging-gateway patterns

    SWIFT, ISO 20022 and FIX adapters that enforce contracts at the boundary and preserve audit copies of every message

  • Feature-flagged, independently deployable

    every build ships behind flags with rehearsed rollback, because the change advisory board will ask

How an engagement runs

The engagement shape is deliberately short on ceremony and long on measurement. It starts with the two-week diagnostic: one workflow baselined end to end, the leak quantified in your own telemetry, the data contracts inventoried, and a written feasibility read you can circulate — including the uncomfortable findings. If the delta is real, the build is scoped against the gates your change and model-risk processes define; if it is not, you keep the measurement design and the baseline either way.

  • Weeks 1-2: diagnostic and baseline

    workflow instrumentation, leak quantification, contract inventory, feasibility read with the delta estimate and the confounders named

  • Weeks 3-8: first vertical slice

    one lane of the workflow live behind flags with replay and evidence running from day one

  • Weeks 9-16: gates, controls, expansion

    model-risk documentation landed, QC sampling live, remaining lanes added, capacity telemetry reporting to the operations review

  • Quarterly: challenger reviews

    threshold and model challengers evaluated in the registry, strategies re-optimized, the baseline re-verified against the control group

Delivery runs in your delivery rhythm, not a parallel one: our engineers work in your repositories or a shared workspace with the same CI, the same security review and the same access model your teams use. Knowledge transfer is part of the gates, not a closing slide — by the expansion phase your engineers are shipping changes to the builds, because the runbooks, contracts and evaluation suites are theirs from week one.

The team and the operating rhythm

A sector build team is small and senior: a lead engineer who has run decision systems in regulated finance, data engineers who own the CDC and contract layer, ML engineers who own scoring and evaluation, a controls engineer who owns the evidence layer, and a domain lead who has cleared KYC backlogs, dispute queues or reconciliation breaks in production estates. Agentic-workflow specialists join for the file-assembly builds, and optimization engineers for rebalancing, collateral and collections. The team works to your release calendar and your gate vocabulary from the first week.

The rhythm is the same at every client: a weekly operations review on the workflow's own telemetry, fortnightly gate submissions to change and model-risk, quarterly challenger reviews, and an incident practice that treats the decision store and the evidence layer as production-critical systems — because by the second quarter, they are. Escalations go to named engineers, not to a queue, and the operations review reads from the same metrics the steering committee reads.

Failure modes we design against

Programs like these fail in known ways, and most of the failure modes are organizational rather than mathematical. We name them in the feasibility read and design against them explicitly, because a program that cannot survive its second quarter is not worth starting. Every one of the six below has a concrete countermeasure that ships as part of the build, not as advice.

  • Pilot purgatory

    a demo built on extracted data that never integrates — prevented by shipping the first vertical slice through your change process, flags and gates included, within two months

  • Model-risk stall

    the assistant or the scoring service stuck in review because documentation was an afterthought — the registry, evaluation results and lineage land with the build, not after it

  • Feature-store fragmentation

    two teams recomputing the same feature with different semantics until decisions diverge — one shared feature store with contract tests is a gate, not an optimization

  • Metric gaming

    containment or approval-rate metrics rising while the customer outcome decays — every headline metric is paired with a counter-metric in the same telemetry

  • Backlog complacency

    alert precision improving while the queue still grows because capacity was cut on a forecast — capacity telemetry stays in the operations review until the curve bends

  • Evidence debt

    audit preparation consuming a team every quarter because artifacts were manual — the evidence layer generates them continuously or the build is not done

Questions banking leaders ask us

  • Do we need to replace our core to start?

    No. None of the builds require a core replacement. They consume streams from your systems of record and write back through the interfaces your change process governs. The core stays; it stops being the bottleneck for decisions.

  • How does this pass model risk review?

    Every generative and scoring component ships with its documentation: data lineage, evaluation results against golden sets, guardrail inventory, drift monitoring and the decision replay store. The review becomes a walkthrough of artifacts the system already produces.

  • Where does the EU AI Act bite first?

    Credit scoring, creditworthiness assessment and risk pricing sit in the high-risk tier, so documentation, human oversight and monitoring attach there first. Our registry and evidence layer were designed for that inventory, and the same artifacts serve SR 11-7-style model-risk frameworks.

  • Can you work with our DORA third-party register?

    Yes. Every integration we ship lands with its register entry, criticality assessment, exit note and test evidence. The tier-1 US bank in our published record moved third-party and incident evidence to automated collection on this pattern.

  • What is the smallest program worth running?

    One workflow, one baseline, one vertical slice. If the diagnostic cannot find the delta in your own telemetry, we say so and stop. The most common starters are onboarding, fraud, disputes, reconciliation or KYC refresh, because their clocks and funnels are already instrumented.

  • How do you measure success without gaming?

    Baselines are locked during the diagnostic, controls or holdouts are used where experiments are cheap, and every metric is reported from system telemetry rather than a status deck. The feasibility read names the confounders before anyone commits to a target.

  • How do the agents avoid becoming shadow AI?

    Agents run inside the governed platform with role scopes, tool allow-lists, human checkpoints and full run logs. The shadow-AI failure mode is what happens without those controls; the program's explicit goal is to eliminate the conditions that create it.

  • Who owns the build after you leave?

    Your teams, with the runbooks, contracts, evaluation suites and registry entries transferred as gate conditions during expansion — not as a closing deliverable. Several programs run entirely in-house after the second quarter.

Bring one workflow from anywhere in the group — a leak you already know by name — and we will baseline it in two weeks, price the delta in your own telemetry, and tell you honestly whether the build is worth your change budget.
