Cybersecurity & Zero-Trust

Cybersecurity & Zero-Trust runs as one control surface, not seven disconnected services: identity, detection, posture, resilience, compliance and AI security share one evidence model — so a control proven once is trusted everywhere, and audit becomes a reading exercise instead of a fire drill.

Security work here is evidence-first engineering: every control has a testable assertion, every detection is validated against a rehearsed attack path, and every exception expires. The domain spans identity, posture, threat intelligence, resilience and AI-specific protection — with the SOC workflow and the audit pack sharing one spine.

Seven practices, one spine: the control register. It tells you what is promised, how it is tested, and what evidence exists today — which is exactly what a regulator, an insurer or a customer's due-diligence team will ask for, in that order.

Seven practices, one evidence model

Identity, zero trust, posture, intel, resilience, model protection and compliance on one surface

Time and evidence — the two currencies of a security program

Containment speed and examinable proof as the design targets

The modern security program is measured in two currencies: time and evidence. Time — how fast identity is revoked, how fast a threat is contained, how fast a misconfiguration is closed. Evidence — what proves each control worked, generated continuously rather than assembled annually. The seven practices here are cut along exactly those two axes.

What makes the program different is the AI layer: prompt-injection defense, corpus integrity, model theft resistance and agent abuse containment are first-class controls in the same gates as firewalls and identity. The estate that runs AI has attack surfaces the classic frameworks never named — and this domain treats them as named, gated, first-class citizens.

    Estate and control surface mapped, gate model scored, detect-to-contain baseline measured on your real telemetry — you keep the scored gate map.

  • Provability sprint — four weeks

Six gates from control assertion to audit pass

Assertion, attack-path, identity, recovery, evidence, exception — each with its evidence

Time and evidence are the two currencies

Containment speed and examinable proof — the two numbers every security decision serves

Programs fail in the space between the two currencies: containment that depends on a hero's timing, and evidence assembled by an annual archaeology project. The architecture closes both gaps — one control surface where identity, device, network and AI-model policies are decided and logged, detection tuned per attack path rather than per product, and the control register as the spine: every control carries an owner, an evidence source and a review date, so compliance is generated continuously rather than reconstructed annually.

The seven practices divide the surface deliberately: identity lifecycle, zero-trust enforcement, posture, threat intelligence, resilience, AI-model protection and compliance engineering. Each can be engaged alone; the evidence model binds them — one register, one evidence collector, one console — because fragmented programs produce the two failure modes that boards actually experience: slow containment (the breach that grew) and audit theater (the report that proved nothing).

  • One control surface

    Identity, device, network and model policies decided and logged in one place.

  • Containment as headline

    Time-to-contain per threat class, drilled and trended.

  • Continuous evidence

    The register collects proof as controls run; audits read, not reconstruct.

  • Attack-path tuning

    Detection mapped to how this estate is actually attacked.

  • We already have a SOC — what changes?

    The detection engineering behind it: coverage measured by attack-path simulation rather than alert counts, containment times drilled and trended per threat class, and the control register that turns SOC output into audit evidence automatically.

  • Is zero trust a product we can buy?

    No — it is an access architecture. Policies are decided on identity, device and context per request; the work is mapping critical paths, placing policy decision points and proving coverage. Products help enforce; they cannot decide for you.

  • How is AI security different from application security?

    The attack surface: prompt injection, corpus poisoning, model theft and agent abuse have no classic CVE taxonomy. The practice treats them as gated controls in the same register — red-team suites, containment drills and evidence, like any other control.

  • What does evidence-first mean for an audit?

    The register generates proof while controls run — configurations, test results, exception expiries. An auditor samples controls and reads artifacts instead of interviewing teams; the first-time pass rate is the metric we sign.

  • Can we start from incident response?

    Yes — resilience is a common entry practice. The first four weeks rehearse recovery against signed RTOs on one business service; the register and detection work then extend from what the rehearsal exposes.

Bring the control your auditor doubts most. The two-week diagnostic will measure its real coverage and evidence trail — and tell you what passing would take, in named owners and hours.
